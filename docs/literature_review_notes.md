# Literature Review 

## Research Focus

My dissertation looks at whether large language models (LLMs) respond differently to mental health questions depending on the geographic location mentioned in the prompt. The project focuses on possible differences in empathy, advice quality, cultural assumptions, and access to mental health support across different countries and cities.

The study compares responses generated by GPT-4o, Claude, and Gemini.

---

# Theme 1 — Large Language Models in Mental Healthcare

### Main papers

* Hua et al. (2025)
* Rousmaniere et al. (2025)
* Mesquiti and Nook (2026)
* Ayers et al. (2023)

### Notes

Recent research shows that people are increasingly using AI systems for emotional support, healthcare advice, and mental health information. Some studies found that chatbot responses can appear supportive and empathetic, and in some situations users even preferred AI-generated answers over human responses.

However, many papers also raise concerns about safety, reliability, hallucinations, and inappropriate advice. Most studies evaluate general healthcare performance rather than differences between geographic or cultural settings.

One important gap is that current research does not explore whether users from lower-resource regions receive weaker or less useful guidance.

---

# Theme 2 — Bias and Fairness in LLMs

### Main papers

* Healey et al. (2024)
* Gupta et al. (2024)
* Omar et al. (2024)
* Li et al. (2024)

### Notes

Several studies show that LLMs can produce biased outputs. Bias may appear through stereotypes, unequal recommendations, or assumptions linked to demographic or cultural information.

Most fairness research focuses on gender, race, or language bias. Geographic bias is discussed much less frequently. This creates an opportunity to investigate whether models respond differently across healthcare infrastructure, economic conditions, and regional contexts.

This area is important for my dissertation because mental health support is strongly affected by access to services and social context.

---

# Theme 3 — Geographic and Cultural Bias

### Main papers

* Decoupes et al. (2025)
* Lyu et al. (2024)
* Jin et al. (2024)
* Orosoo et al. (2024)

### Notes

Research in this area suggests that language models may reflect regional patterns found in their training data. Some papers show that model performance changes across languages, locations, and cultural settings.

Cross-lingual healthcare studies also found that models often perform better in English or in high-resource contexts. This may create unequal quality in healthcare advice.

Very little work has specifically examined mental health responses across cities or countries, creating a significant research gap for the dissertation.

---

# Theme 4 — Mental Health Access and Inequality

### Main papers

* Osborn et al. (2024)
* Başar and Öztürk (2020)
* Zhao et al. (2020)

### Notes

Access to mental healthcare is unequal across countries and regions. Some areas have strong healthcare systems and professional support, while others face shortages, high costs, or social stigma around mental illness.

This is important because LLMs may unintentionally assume that therapy, crisis services, or healthcare professionals are easily available everywhere.

Part of this dissertation will examine whether models recognise these differences or give unrealistic recommendations.

---

# Theme 5 — Evaluation Methods

### Main papers

* Dai et al. (2023)
* De Paoli (2024)
* Golden and Aboujaoude (2024)

### Notes

Many recent studies combine qualitative coding with NLP analysis to evaluate AI-generated text. Some papers also discuss the limitations of using LLMs in thematic analysis.

For this dissertation, I plan to combine:

* structured human coding,
* comparative scoring,
* thematic analysis,
* and NLP-based evaluation methods.

This mixed-method approach should make the analysis more balanced and reliable.

---

# Current Research Gap

Most current research looks at:

* general healthcare performance,
* medical accuracy,
* or broad fairness issues.

There is still limited research on:

* geographic variation in mental health responses,
* cultural assumptions in AI advice,
* and whether LLMs provide different levels of support depending on location.

This dissertation aims to address that gap.
