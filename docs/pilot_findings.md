# Pilot Findings

## Overview

A pilot dataset consisting of 36 responses was collected using six mental health prompts, two geographic locations (London, UK and Lagos, Nigeria), and three large language models (GPT-4o, Gemini, and Claude).

The pilot was conducted to explore whether geographic context influences mental health guidance generated by large language models and to identify preliminary themes for subsequent thematic analysis.

---

## Geographic Differences

Across all six prompts, responses varied depending on the location specified in each prompt.

London responses consistently referenced:

* NHS Talking Therapies
* GP referrals
* NHS 111
* Samaritans
* Local councils
* Welfare and employment support systems
* Formal caregiver services

In contrast, Lagos' responses more frequently referenced:

* Mentally Aware Nigeria Initiative (MANI)
* SURPIN
* Federal Neuro-Psychiatric Hospital Yaba
* Lagos University Teaching Hospital (LUTH)
* Family support networks
* Religious communities
* Community-based support structures

These findings suggest that all three models adapted recommendations according to local healthcare systems and available support infrastructure.

---

## Model Differences

Although all models demonstrated geographic adaptation, differences in response style were observed.

### GPT-4o

GPT-4o frequently focused on:

* Behavioural strategies
* Self-management techniques
* Structured action plans
* Risk assessment

Responses often encouraged users to build routines, track symptoms, and focus on practical behavioural changes.

### Gemini

Gemini produced the most localised responses.

Characteristics included:

* Detailed lists of local organisations
* Specific referral pathways
* Step-by-step action plans
* Extensive contextual information

Gemini consistently provided the most location-specific resources.

### Claude

Claude generally produced shorter responses.

Characteristics included:

* Balanced emotional support
* Clear referral recommendations
* Practical advice
* Direct communication style

Claude's responses tended to focus on immediate actions and professional support options.

---

## Preliminary Themes

Ten preliminary themes emerged from the pilot dataset:

1. Institutional Healthcare Navigation
2. Support Under Resource Constraints
3. Behavioural Self-Management
4. Localised Resource Referral
5. Professional Mental Health Referral
6. Community Integration and Belonging
7. Financial Security and Economic Resilience
8. Caregiver Burden and Emotional Exhaustion
9. Formal vs Informal Support Systems
10. Integrated Physical and Mental Health Care

These themes appeared repeatedly across prompts and models, suggesting they may form a stable coding framework for the full study.

---

## Preliminary Interpretation

The pilot findings indicate that geographic context influences not only healthcare recommendations but also broader assumptions regarding social support, economic resilience, caregiving responsibilities, and access to mental health services.

Furthermore, model-specific characteristics remained relatively stable across all prompts. Gemini demonstrated the strongest localisation, GPT-4o emphasised behavioural guidance and structured coping strategies, and Claude provided concise and balanced recommendations.

These findings provide initial evidence that both geographic context and model characteristics shape the mental health guidance generated by large language models.
