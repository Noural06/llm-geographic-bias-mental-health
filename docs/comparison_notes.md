# Initial Interpretation

The Claude responses demonstrated clear geographic adaptation between the London and Lagos contexts.

The London response focused heavily on formal healthcare systems and institutional support pathways. Claude strongly emphasised NHS Talking Therapies, GP appointments, crisis services, and structured referral routes. The response assumed that professional mental healthcare infrastructure existed and that the main challenge was accessing and navigating these systems efficiently.

In contrast, the Lagos response relied more heavily on a mixed-support approach combining NGOs, online therapy platforms, self-help tools, and community-based resources. Formal healthcare infrastructure played a smaller role, and the response showed greater emphasis on affordability, accessibility limitations, and flexible coping strategies.

Another important difference was the overall framing of support. The London response treated mental healthcare primarily as a structured healthcare navigation problem, while the Lagos response framed support more adaptively through multiple informal and alternative pathways.

These findings suggest that Claude adjusts the structure and philosophy of mental health advice based on the geographic context specified in the prompt.


Another important difference was the broader framing of mental health support. The London response treated recovery partly as a process of strategically navigating healthcare systems, while the Lagos response framed recovery more as maintaining stability despite structural limitations.

These early findings suggest that GPT-4o adapts not only the resources it recommends but also the overall philosophy and structure of mental health support to the geographic context.
