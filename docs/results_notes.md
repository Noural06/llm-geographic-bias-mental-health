# Results Notes

## Theme 1: Institutional Healthcare Navigation

### Observation

Institutional Healthcare Navigation emerged as one of the most prominent themes across the dataset. Responses frequently directed users towards formal healthcare systems, professional medical services, and structured referral pathways.

### Evidence

Across all three models, London-based responses commonly referred users to:

* General Practitioners (GPs)
* NHS Talking Therapies
* NHS 111 Mental Health Services
* Specialist clinics
* Hospital-based care

Examples included recommendations to self-refer to NHS Talking Therapies, schedule GP appointments, and access NHS mental health services for ongoing support.

Lagos-based responses also referred users to professional healthcare services, but typically recommended:

* Federal Neuro-Psychiatric Hospital, Yaba
* Lagos University Teaching Hospital (LUTH)
* Lagos State University Teaching Hospital (LASUTH)
* Local psychiatrists and psychologists

However, these referrals were often accompanied by alternative support options due to perceived accessibility constraints.

### Geographic Differences

London responses generally assumed the availability of structured healthcare pathways and emphasised formal referral systems.

In contrast, Lagos responses frequently acknowledged potential barriers to accessing healthcare and supplemented institutional referrals with alternative support mechanisms.

### Preliminary Interpretation

The findings suggest that all three models adapt to local healthcare environments when implementing healthcare recommendations. Formal healthcare systems were more strongly emphasised in London responses, while Lagos responses balanced institutional recommendations with alternative support pathways.

## Theme 2: Support Under Resource Constraints

### Observation

Support Under Resource Constraints emerged primarily in Lagos responses and was significantly less common in London responses.

### Evidence

Lagos responses are frequently discussed:

* Limited access to mental health services
* Financial barriers to care
* Availability of low-cost support options
* Community-based assistance
* Teletherapy and online services

Several responses recommended NGOs, peer-support organisations, faith communities, and informal support networks as practical alternatives when professional services were difficult to access.

London responses rarely discussed limitations in service availability and generally assumed access to formal healthcare pathways.

### Geographic Differences

The contrast between locations was particularly evident within this theme.

London responses focused on navigating existing healthcare systems, whereas Lagos responses frequently acknowledged resource limitations and proposed alternative forms of support.

### Preliminary Interpretation

These findings suggest that models incorporate assumptions about healthcare infrastructure and service accessibility when generating responses. Geographic context appears to influence not only the resources recommended but also the perceived availability of professional support.

## Theme 3: Localised Resource Referral

### Observation

All three models demonstrated localisation by recommending services, organisations, and resources specific to the user's stated location.

### Evidence

London responses frequently referred users to:

* NHS Talking Therapies
* NHS 111
* Samaritans
* Mind UK
* Citizens Advice
* Carers UK

Lagos responses frequently referred users to:

* Mentally Aware Nigeria Initiative (MANI)
* Suicide Research and Prevention Initiative (SURPIN)
* Federal Neuro-Psychiatric Hospital, Yaba
* LUTH
* LASUTH
* Lagos Lifeline

Gemini generally provided the most extensive lists of local services and support organisations. Claude tended to provide concise but targeted referrals. GPT-4o included local resources but often placed greater emphasis on behavioural strategies and structured action plans.

### Geographic Differences

Localisation occurred consistently across all models; however, the degree of localisation varied substantially. London responses primarily highlighted formal institutional services, whereas Lagos responses incorporated a mixture of institutional, community-based, and NGO resources.

### Preliminary Interpretation

The consistent presence of location-specific recommendations indicates that all models can adapt support resources to the geographic context. However, the extent and nature of localisation differ between models.
## Theme 4: Behavioural Self-Management

### Observation

Behavioural Self-Management was one of the most consistently occurring themes across all prompts and models. Responses frequently encouraged users to adopt practical strategies aimed at improving mental wellbeing through changes in behaviour, routine, and daily habits.

### Evidence

Common recommendations included:

* Regular physical exercise
* Sleep hygiene practices
* Maintaining daily routines
* Symptom tracking
* Breathing exercises
* Grounding techniques
* Reducing social media consumption
* Increasing social interaction

GPT-4o demonstrated the strongest emphasis on behavioural strategies. Its responses often provided structured action plans, prioritised daily routines, and encouraged users to focus on controllable behaviours rather than emotional states.

Gemini and Claude also included behavioural recommendations but generally balanced these with referrals to professional services and support organisations.

### Geographic Differences

Behavioural self-management strategies appeared in both London and Lagos responses. However, Lagos responses often presented behavioural recommendations as practical alternatives when access to professional services might be limited.

### Preliminary Interpretation

The widespread use of behavioural recommendations suggests that all models consider self-management strategies to be an important component of mental health support. GPT-4o placed particularly strong emphasis on behavioural intervention, indicating a distinctive model-specific response style.

## Theme 5: Professional Mental Health Referral

### Observation

Professional Mental Health Referral emerged consistently across the dataset. Responses frequently encouraged users to seek support from qualified healthcare professionals when symptoms were persistent, severe, or significantly affecting daily functioning.

### Evidence

Typical recommendations included:

* Consulting a GP
* Speaking with a psychologist
* Accessing counselling services
* Seeking psychiatric assessment
* Attending specialist clinics
* Using NHS Talking Therapies
* Contacting mental health organisations

Professional referrals were particularly prominent in prompts involving anxiety, depression, panic attacks, chronic insomnia, and caregiver burden.

Claude demonstrated a strong tendency to recommend professional services early in its responses, while Gemini often supplemented professional referrals with detailed information about available local services.

GPT-4o generally balanced professional referral with behavioural strategies and self-management advice.

### Geographic Differences

Although professional referral occurred in both locations, the specific pathways differed substantially.

London responses focused on:

* GPs
* NHS Talking Therapies
* NHS Mental Health Services

Lagos responses focused on:

* Psychiatric hospitals
* Teaching hospitals
* Mental health organisations
* Local counselling services

### Preliminary Interpretation

The findings suggest that all models recognise the importance of professional intervention for significant mental health concerns. However, the recommended pathways vary according to geographic context and healthcare infrastructure.

## Theme 6: Community Integration and Belonging

* Joining hobby groups
* Sports clubs
* Community events
* Faith-based organisations
* Alumni associations

Gemini provided extensive lists of community resources and social activities, while Claude focused on practical and accessible opportunities for connection.

### Geographic Differences

London responses frequently referred users to:

* Meetup groups
* Volunteering organisations
* Community centres
* Social applications such as Bumble BFF

Lagos responses more commonly referred users to:

* Faith-based communities
* Alumni networks
* Professional groups
* Community organisations
* Local social networks

### Preliminary Interpretation

Models appear to adapt recommendations according to perceived cultural and social structures. London responses emphasised organised social activities, while Lagos responses placed greater emphasis on existing community networks, family connections, and religious organisations.

## Theme 8: Caregiver Burden and Emotional Exhaustion

### Observation

Caregiver Burden and Emotional Exhaustion emerged primarily in Prompt 5. Responses consistently recognised the emotional, psychological, and physical strain associated with long-term caregiving responsibilities.

### Evidence

Common recommendations included:

* Seeking emotional support
* Prioritising personal wellbeing
* Accessing counselling services
* Sharing caregiving responsibilities

Gemini provided the most detailed guidance, including information about caregiver assessments, support organisations, and respite services.

GPT-4o focused on recognising burnout and encouraging sustainable caregiving practices.

Claude provided concise recommendations centred on practical support and emotional wellbeing.

### Geographic Differences

London responses frequently referred to:

* Carer's Assessments
* Local council services
* Carers UK
* NHS support pathways

Lagos responses more commonly referred to:

* Family support networks
* Religious communities
* Community assistance
* Mental health organisations
* Teaching hospitals and psychiatric services

### Preliminary Interpretation

The findings suggest that models adapt caregiver support recommendations according to the availability of formal support structures. London responses emphasised institutional support, while Lagos responses relied more heavily on community and family-based support systems.

## Theme 9: Formal vs Informal Support Systems

### Observation

A recurring pattern across the dataset was the distinction between formal institutional support and informal community-based support.

### Evidence

Formal support systems included:

* NHS services
* Hospitals
* Government agencies
* Mental health professionals
* Local councils
* Structured referral pathways

Informal support systems included:

* Family members
* Friends
* Religious organisations
* Community groups
* Peer-support networks

London responses generally prioritised formal systems as the first line of support.

Lagos responses often combined formal and informal systems, frequently recommending family members, community leaders, faith communities, and local support networks alongside professional services.

### Geographic Differences

The contrast between locations was particularly pronounced within this theme.

London responses commonly assumed that professional support services would be accessible and appropriate.

Lagos responses frequently incorporated informal support structures into recommended coping strategies and care pathways.

### Preliminary Interpretation

These findings suggest that models make assumptions regarding available support infrastructures and social norms. Geographic context appears to influence not only healthcare recommendations but also expectations regarding family, community, and institutional support.

## Theme 10: Integrated Physical and Mental Health Care

### Observation

Integrated Physical and Mental Health Care emerged most clearly in Prompt 6, which focused on chronic insomnia. Responses frequently recognised the interaction between physical health conditions and psychological wellbeing.

### Evidence

Common recommendations included:

* Medical assessment
* Sleep diaries
* Sleep hygiene practices
* Cognitive Behavioural Therapy for Insomnia (CBT-I)
* Mental health assessment
* Specialist sleep services

Responses frequently highlighted the relationship between:

* Sleep quality
* Anxiety
* Depression
* Stress
* Daily functioning

Gemini provided the most comprehensive explanations regarding sleep disorders and specialist care pathways.

GPT-4o emphasised assessment, symptom monitoring, and evidence-based treatment approaches.

Claude focused on concise clinical recommendations and practical sleep-management strategies.

### Geographic Differences

London responses commonly referred users to:

* GPs
* NHS Talking Therapies
* NHS Sleep Clinics
* CBT-I programmes

Lagos responses referred users to:

* General practitioners
* Psychiatric services
* Sleep specialists where available
* Mental health organisations

Lagos responses were also more likely to discuss environmental factors affecting sleep, including heat, noise, and infrastructure-related challenges.

### Preliminary Interpretation

The findings indicate that models recognise the close relationship between physical and mental health. Geographic context influences both the referral pathways recommended and the environmental factors considered relevant to the user's situation.

---

## Overall Findings

Across all six prompts, the analysis identified ten preliminary themes.

The results suggest that all three models adapt responses according to geographic context. However, the degree and nature of adaptation vary considerably between models.

Gemini demonstrated the strongest localisation, frequently providing extensive lists of local services, organisations, and support resources.

GPT-4 consistently emphasised behavioural self-management, structured action plans, and practical decision-making frameworks.

Claude generally provided balanced responses that combined professional referral, emotional support, and practical guidance.

Across the dataset, London responses relied more heavily on formal healthcare systems and institutional support pathways. Lagos responses more frequently incorporated community resources, family support structures, NGOs, and accessibility-aware recommendations.

These findings provide preliminary evidence that geographic context influences not only healthcare referrals but also broader assumptions regarding resource availability, social support, economic resilience, and mental health care pathways.
