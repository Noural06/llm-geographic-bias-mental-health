# Do LLMs Provide Reliable Local Mental-Health Guidance?

## Evaluating Geographic Variation and Crisis Contacts

MSc Data Science dissertation project, Middlesex University, London, 2026.

This repository contains the data, code, validation outputs, figures and tables for a controlled geographic audit of mental-health guidance generated by large language models.

## Study design

The main corpus varies only the stated city and country while holding the English-language mental-health scenario constant.

| Design element | Number |
|---|---:|
| Cities | 20 |
| Scenarios | 8 |
| Models | 7 |
| Main-corpus responses | 1,120 |

The primary coding method is a transparent rule-based NLP pipeline using frozen regular expressions and vocabularies. Prompted Qwen coding was evaluated only as a diagnostic and did not generate the final RQ1–RQ3 outcomes.

## Evidential status

| Research question | Final status |
|---|---|
| RQ1: Actionability and income | **Exploratory.** The fresh hold-out measure failed validation; the apparent gradient is driven mainly by crisis-contact inclusion and becomes non-significant after service availability is included. |
| RQ2: Localisation and documented service availability | **Exploratory.** Surface localisation failed the fresh hold-out specificity criterion. Surface and verified localisation have the same coefficient direction, but neither supports confirmation. |
| RQ3: Religious recommendations and WHO region | **Cautiously retained.** Separate validation produced F1 = 0.889, subject to single-coder assessment. |

The study reports location-conditioned associations within the selected cities. It does not claim global bias, causation, stereotyping or a fabrication rate.

## Independent human coding

A second coder independently labelled a blinded 40-response subset. Only social support met the locked reliability threshold of κ ≥ 0.70. Agreement remained below threshold for overall actionability (weighted κ = 0.134), surface localisation (κ = 0.286) and verified localisation (κ = 0.548). H1 and H2 therefore remain exploratory.

## Surface and verified localisation

- **Surface localisation** records apparent local content, including a place, institution or contact.
- **Verified localisation** gives contact credit only when the country–number pair matches a documented service.
- A plausible-looking number is not treated as verified merely because its format appears local.

## Crisis-contact audit

The main audit contains 466 records corresponding to 417 unique country–number pairs. Each record is kept in one of five categories:

1. verified real;
2. general emergency;
3. verified incorrect;
4. visibly suspicious;
5. unresolved.

Unresolved does not mean fake or incorrect.

## Supplementary generation-stability test

The completed stability test contains 54 new responses:

```text
3 cities × 2 scenarios × 3 accessible Groq models × 3 repetitions = 54
```

| Outcome | Completely stable cells | Pairwise agreement |
|---|---:|---:|
| Actionability | 9/18 | 64.8% |
| Surface localisation | 18/18 | 100.0% |
| Verified localisation | 15/18 | 88.9% |
| Crisis-contact inclusion | 18/18 | 100.0% |
| Religious recommendation | 16/18 | 92.6% |

The test assesses generation consistency only. It does not repair the failed H1/H2 measurement validation. Two original endpoints were unavailable, replacement models were used, GPT-OSS-20B required a higher completion ceiling, and temperature was 0.0. These constraints make the test supplementary rather than an exact replication of the original experiment.

The separate stability-sample contact audit found 19 unique country–number pairs: 2 verified real, 5 general emergency, 4 verified incorrect, 7 visibly suspicious and 1 unresolved. These results are not pooled with the main 466-record audit.

6. Interpret H1 and H2 only as exploratory.

## Final limitations

- Only 20 deliberately selected cities were studied.
- Income, WHO region and service infrastructure are correlated.
- Prompts were English-only.
- Most H1/H2 human-coded outcomes failed the reliability threshold.
- H3 remains single-coded.
- Contact-verification depth is uneven across countries.
- The stability experiment used replacement endpoints, unequal completion ceilings and deterministic temperature.
- The study evaluates model response content, not clinical effectiveness.

## Ethical scope

All prompts are synthetic. No patients or service users participated. Model outputs must not be treated as medical advice or as a verified crisis-service directory.
