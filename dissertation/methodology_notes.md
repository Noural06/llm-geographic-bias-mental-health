# Methodology Notes

## Research Design

This study adopts a qualitative comparative research design to investigate whether geographic context influences responses generated by large language models (LLMs) to mental health-related queries. The study compares responses from multiple LLMs when presented with identical prompts that differ only by user location.

## Models

The following large language models were evaluated:

* GPT-4o
* Gemini
* Claude

These models were selected because they are among the most widely used general-purpose LLMs and are commonly used to provide information and advice on health-related topics.

## Locations

Two geographic locations were selected for the pilot study:

* London, United Kingdom
* Lagos, Nigeria

These locations were chosen because they represent different healthcare systems, levels of mental health service availability, and socioeconomic contexts.

## Prompt Design

Six standardised mental health prompts were developed:

1. Anxiety and Depression
2. Panic Attacks
3. Loneliness and Social Isolation
4. Job Loss and Financial Stress
5. Caregiver Burden
6. Chronic Insomnia

Each prompt described a common mental health scenario and included an explicit geographic location.

## Data Collection

Each prompt was submitted to all three models using both geographic locations.

This produced:

* 6 prompts
* 2 locations
* 3 models

Total responses collected:

* 36 responses

All responses were manually collected and stored for subsequent analysis.

## Data Analysis

### Primary Analysis Method

The primary analytical approach is Reflexive Thematic Analysis following Braun and Clarke (2006, 2019).

Thematic analysis was selected because it enables the systematic identification and interpretation of recurring patterns within text-based data.

### Secondary Analysis

Additional comparisons were conducted to examine:

* Differences between models
* Differences between geographic locations
* Variations in localisation strategies
* Variations in referral pathways and support recommendations

## Coding Procedure

The analysis followed the following stages:

1. Familiarisation with the dataset.
2. Initial coding of responses.
3. Identification of recurring patterns.
4. Development of a preliminary codebook.
5. Construction of a coding matrix.
6. Refinement of themes.
7. Cross-model comparison.
8. Cross-location comparison.

## Pilot Study Outcomes

The pilot dataset yielded 36 responses and identified 10 preliminary themes.

These themes form the basis for the full thematic analysis phase and will be refined as additional data are collected.
