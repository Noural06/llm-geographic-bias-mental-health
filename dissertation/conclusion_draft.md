# \section{Conclusion}

This dissertation investigated geographic variation in mental health responses generated by large language models. Using a comparative qualitative design, responses produced by GPT-4o, Gemini, and Claude were analysed across six standardised mental health scenarios presented from two geographic contexts: London (United Kingdom) and Lagos (Nigeria).

The findings demonstrated that all three models adjusted their responses to the geographic context. Differences were observed in healthcare referrals, support recommendations, crisis pathways, assumptions about community support, and resource accessibility. London-based responses frequently emphasised formal healthcare services and institutional support structures, whereas Lagos-based responses more commonly referenced specialist hospitals, community organisations, family networks, and accessibility-aware recommendations.

The study also identified clear differences between models. Gemini demonstrated the strongest localisation behaviour, GPT-4o consistently emphasised behavioural self-management strategies, and Claude adopted a balanced and referral-oriented approach. These findings suggest that both geographic context and model-specific characteristics influence the generation of mental health guidance.

Overall, the results provide preliminary evidence that large language models do not generate entirely location-neutral responses. Instead, they incorporate assumptions regarding healthcare infrastructure, social support systems, and resource availability when constructing recommendations.

## \section{Contributions}

This dissertation makes three primary contributions.

First, it contributes empirical evidence regarding geographic variation in AI-generated mental health responses. While previous research has explored demographic and linguistic bias in large language models, relatively little work has focused specifically on geographic context within mental health support scenarios.

Second, the study contributes a methodological framework for examining geographic adaptation through qualitative thematic analysis. The combination of prompt-based evaluation, thematic coding, and cross-model comparison provides a practical approach for investigating contextual variation in AI-generated outputs.

Third, the findings contribute to broader discussions of AI fairness, responsible deployment, and digital mental health by highlighting how location-specific assumptions can influence the advice and support pathways generated.

## \section{Further Work}

Several opportunities exist for extending this research.

Future studies should examine additional geographic locations representing a wider range of cultural, economic, and healthcare environments. Expanding the dataset would enable stronger conclusions about the prevalence and consistency of geographic adaptation across contexts.

Further research could also incorporate quantitative evaluation measures, including referral accuracy, response specificity, actionability, and crisis-support effectiveness. Combining qualitative and quantitative approaches may provide a more comprehensive understanding of geographic variation.

Additional work could investigate user perceptions of geographically adapted responses and evaluate whether such adaptations improve user outcomes or contribute to unequal access to mental health guidance.

Finally, future studies should continue to monitor newer generations of large language models as their capabilities evolve and deployment in healthcare contexts becomes increasingly widespread.
