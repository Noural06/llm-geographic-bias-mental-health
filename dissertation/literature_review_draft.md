# Geographic Variation in Large Language Model Responses to Mental Health Queries.


## Chapter 2: Literature Review

### 2.1 Large Language Models in Healthcare

Large Language Models (LLMs) have rapidly emerged as important tools within healthcare settings. Recent advances in transformer-based architectures have enabled models such as GPT-4o, Gemini, and Claude to generate human-like responses across a wide range of medical and health-related tasks. Research has demonstrated that LLMs can support clinical decision-making, patient education, medical documentation, and health information retrieval (Singhal et al., 2023; Wang & Zhang, 2024).

One of the most significant developments in this area was the introduction of Med-PaLM, which demonstrated that large language models can encode substantial amounts of clinical knowledge and perform competitively on medical benchmark examinations (Singhal et al., 2023). These findings suggest that LLMs have the potential to augment healthcare delivery and improve access to medical information.

Despite these opportunities, concerns remain regarding reliability, hallucinations, safety, fairness, and bias. Busch et al. (2025) identified several challenges associated with deploying LLMs in patient care, including misinformation, lack of transparency, and inconsistent recommendations. Consequently, there is increasing interest in evaluating how these systems behave across different healthcare contexts and populations.

## 2.2 Large Language Models in Mental Health

Mental health has emerged as one of the most common domains in which individuals seek information and support from conversational AI systems. Users frequently interact with LLMs regarding anxiety, depression, loneliness, stress, panic attacks, and other psychological concerns.

A recent scoping review by Hua et al. (2025) found that LLMs are increasingly being explored as tools for mental health support, screening, psychoeducation, and therapeutic assistance. Similarly, Rousmaniere et al. (2025) reported growing public use of LLMs as informal mental health resources.

Research has also compared AI-generated responses with human-generated mental health support. Ayers et al. (2023) found that AI-generated healthcare responses were frequently rated higher in quality and empathy than physician responses in an online setting. Saha et al. (2025) further demonstrated linguistic differences between AI-generated and human-written responses to mental health queries.

Although these findings suggest potential benefits, concerns remain about clinical safety, ethical implications, and the appropriateness of advice provided in vulnerable situations. Santos et al. (2026) emphasised the importance of evaluating safety when LLMs respond to high-risk mental health disclosures.

## 2.3 Bias in Large Language Models

Bias has become one of the most widely discussed challenges associated with LLM deployment. Previous studies have identified demographic, cultural, linguistic, and socioeconomic biases within language model outputs.

Healey et al. (2024) demonstrated that nuanced forms of bias can emerge even when models provide seemingly neutral responses. Similarly, Gupta et al. (2024) highlighted the need for transparency and bias mitigation mechanisms within LLM systems.

Within healthcare applications, concerns regarding fairness are particularly important because biased recommendations may contribute to unequal access to information and support. Li et al. (2024) argue that artificial intelligence systems must be carefully evaluated to ensure they do not reinforce existing health inequities.

The literature suggests that fairness and bias assessment should be considered a central component of healthcare AI evaluation.
## 2.4 Geographic Bias in Large Language Models

While substantial research has examined demographic and linguistic bias in large language models, geographic bias remains relatively underexplored. Geographic bias refers to systematic differences in model outputs arising from a user's location, region, or national context.

Recent studies suggest that language models may produce different responses when geographic information is altered. Decoupes et al. (2025) identified geographical distortions within language models, demonstrating that outputs can vary significantly depending on the region referenced in a prompt. Their findings suggest that models may encode assumptions regarding economic development, healthcare infrastructure, social norms, and institutional capacity.

Similarly, Lyu et al. (2024) found evidence of regional bias within English-language models. The authors observed systematic variation in model behaviour across geographic regions despite prompts being written in the same language. These findings indicate that geographic context may influence recommendations, information retrieval, and response framing.

Research examining demographic disparities in medical language models has produced similar concerns. Omar et al. (2024) reported that medical LLMs can generate different recommendations depending on users' demographic characteristics and the contextual information they provide. Although geographic location was not the primary focus of their study, the findings suggest that contextual attributes may influence healthcare guidance.

Cross-cultural communication research further indicates that language models adapt responses according to cultural expectations and local contexts (Orosoo et al., 2024). While such adaptation may improve relevance, it may also introduce inconsistencies in information quality and accessibility across different populations.

Despite growing interest in fairness and bias within artificial intelligence systems, relatively little research has specifically investigated geographic variation in mental health responses generated by large language models. Existing studies have primarily focused on demographic bias, linguistic bias, or general healthcare applications. Consequently, there remains a limited understanding of how geographic context influences mental health guidance provided by contemporary LLMs.

This gap is particularly important because mental health support often depends heavily on local healthcare systems, available resources, cultural expectations, and social support structures. As a result, geographic adaptation may influence not only referral recommendations but also the broader framing of mental health advice.

The present study addresses this gap by examining whether large language models provide different mental health responses when the same prompt is presented from different geographic locations.
## 2.6 Research Gap and Study Contribution

The existing literature demonstrates growing interest in the application of large language models within healthcare and mental health settings. Previous studies have examined the clinical capabilities of LLMs, their use as mental health resources, and various forms of demographic, linguistic, and cultural bias (Ayers et al., 2023; Hua et al., 2025; Rousmaniere et al., 2025).

Research has also highlighted concerns regarding fairness and bias in healthcare-related AI systems. Studies have identified demographic disparities, regional bias, and geographical distortions in language model outputs (Lyu et al., 2024; Decoupes et al., 2025; Omar et al., 2024). These findings suggest that user-provided contextual information may influence the responses generated by LLMs.

However, several important gaps remain within the current literature.

First, relatively little research has specifically examined geographic variation in mental health responses generated by contemporary large language models. Existing studies have largely focused on general healthcare applications, demographic bias, or multilingual performance rather than mental health support.

Second, there is limited comparative evidence regarding how different leading models respond to identical mental health scenarios when geographic context is manipulated. Most previous studies evaluate a single model or focus on benchmark performance rather than comparing real-world user interactions across multiple systems.

Third, few studies have examined how geographic context influences not only healthcare referrals but also broader assumptions about social support, community resources, economic resilience, caregiving responsibilities, and the accessibility of mental health services.

To address these gaps, this dissertation investigates whether mental health responses generated by GPT-4o, Gemini, and Claude vary across geographic contexts. Using standardised mental health prompts presented from London (United Kingdom) and Lagos (Nigeria), the study examines differences in referral pathways, support strategies, resource recommendations, and thematic content.

The study contributes to the emerging literature on geographic bias in large language models by providing a comparative qualitative analysis of mental health responses across multiple models and locations. In doing so, it offers insights into how geographic context may shape the information and guidance provided by AI systems in sensitive healthcare-related situations.
