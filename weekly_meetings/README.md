# Does Where You Are Shape What You Get?

## Geographic Variation in Large Language Model Responses to Mental-Health Queries

MSc Data Science dissertation project, Middlesex University London, 2026.

This repository contains the data, code, validation outputs, figures, tables and dissertation materials for a controlled geographic audit of mental-health guidance generated by seven large language models.

## Study overview

The experiment varies the stated city and country while holding the English-language mental-health scenario constant.

| Design element | Number | Reason |
|---|---:|---|
| Cities | 20 | Five cities from each World Bank income group, spanning six WHO regions. This provides balanced geographic comparison while keeping the complete factorial design and manual audit feasible. It does not represent every city globally. |
| Scenarios | 8 | Covers different mental-health needs and acuity levels so the findings are not determined by one prompt. |
| Models | 7 | Tests whether location-conditioned differences appear across model families rather than in one model only. |
| Responses | 1,120 | One response for every city × scenario × model cell: 20 × 8 × 7. The provenance audit found all expected cells, no failed cells and no duplicated response text. |

The primary extraction method is a transparent rule-based NLP pipeline implemented with fixed regular expressions and controlled vocabularies. A prompted `Qwen/Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct` classifier was evaluated only as a measurement-development diagnostic. It was not used to generate the final RQ1–RQ3 outcomes because its validity was not established.

## Research questions and final evidential status

| Research question | Outcome and model | Final status |
|---|---|---|
| RQ1: Does actionability vary by income group? | `actionability_v2` (0–5); mixed-effects model controlling for model and scenario, with city random intercept | **Exploratory.** The fresh hold-out validation failed. The observed income gradient is driven mainly by crisis-contact inclusion and becomes non-significant after documented service availability is included. |
| RQ2: Does localisation vary with documented crisis-service availability? | `localisation_v2c` (0–2); mixed-effects model with model/scenario controls and city random intercept | **Exploratory.** The fresh hold-out validation failed because specificity was 0.038. Surface and verified localisation have the same coefficient direction, but this is sensitivity evidence rather than confirmation. |
| RQ3: Does religious recommendation vary by WHO region? | `religious_rec` (0/1); Firth penalised logistic regression controlling for model and scenario | **Cautiously retained.** Separate validation produced F1 = 0.889, 95% CI [0.678, 0.934], subject to the single-coder limitation. |

The study therefore does **not** claim that income, region or culture causally determines a response. It reports location-conditioned associations within the selected 20 cities.

## Response to supervisor feedback

This section answers the supervisor feedback dated **4 August 2026**. “Completed” means the requested analysis or documentation is present. It does not mean the resulting measure passed validation.

### 1. Reduce the study to clear outcome families and hypotheses

**Feedback:** Replace diffuse exploratory indicators with a small number of explicitly defined outcomes and connect each hypothesis to its measure.

**Response: Completed.**

- The final study is organised around actionability, localisation and religious recommendation.
- Response length is retained as exploratory context rather than a primary outcome.
- Generic sentiment and the failed single-winner support-orientation outcome were removed from confirmatory interpretation.
- Each research question now identifies its outcome, geographic predictor, controls, statistical model, interpretation and limitation.

**Evidence:** final dissertation Methodology and Results; `tables/Table_08_NLPFeatureCodebook.csv`; `tables/Table_09_StatisticalMethodsCodebook.csv`.

### 2. Define every metric and extraction rule precisely

**Feedback:** Document definitions, regex or vocabulary rules, recommendation context, examples, failure modes and validation results.

**Response: Completed.**

- `actionability_v2` is the sum of five binary components: crisis contact, directive professional referral, emergency escalation, immediate-action language and named coping step.
- `localisation_v2c = min(explicit location + named institution + contact present, 2)`.
- `religious_rec` requires religious/spiritual vocabulary and directive recommendation context.
- The dissertation appendices document positive examples, negative examples, known failure cases and validation performance.
- Qwen `<think>...</think>` reasoning blocks are removed before feature extraction so hidden reasoning is not counted as visible advice.

**Evidence:** `tables/Table_08_NLPFeatureCodebook.csv`; `tables/Table_12_ExtractionValidation.csv`; `tables/Table_13_RevisedValidation.csv`; dissertation Appendix C.

### 3. Validate the automated measurements against human judgement

**Feedback:** Manually label a stratified sample, report precision, recall and F1, and revise or discard weak features.

**Response: Completed, with important failures reported.**

- Development labels were used to rebuild actionability and localisation after their original versions failed.
- The rebuilt rules were frozen and tested on a fresh, blinded 160-response hold-out balanced across all 20 cities, eight scenarios, four income groups and seven models.
- Actionability failed the locked criteria: weighted κ = 0.462; high-actionability F1 = 0.757; sensitivity = 0.624.
- Surface localisation also failed: F1 = 0.907 but specificity = 0.038 because almost every response was classified as localised.
- Religious recommendation was assessed separately and achieved F1 = 0.889.
- A blinded 40-response repeat-coding exercise found that none of eight outcomes reached κ ≥ 0.70. This demonstrates instability in the single-coder reference standard.

**Decision:** H1 and H2 are explicitly labelled exploratory. The hold-out labels were not used to repair the frozen rules after failure. H3 is retained cautiously. A future replication requires a clearer codebook and independent coders.

**Evidence:** `data/processed/`; `tables/Table_12_ExtractionValidation.csv`; `tables/Table_13_RevisedValidation.csv`; `tables/Validation_Error_Audit.csv`; `src/analyse_holdout.py`; `src/calculate_repeat_reliability.py`.

### 4. Retain city-level detail and use controlled statistical models

**Feedback:** Do not aggregate away city, model or prompt identifiers; analyse geographic predictors with suitable controls and city-level robustness checks.

**Response: Completed.**

- Response-, city-, scenario- and model-level identifiers are retained.
- H1 and H2 use response-level mixed-effects models with model and scenario controls and a city random intercept.
- H3 uses Firth penalised logistic regression because the European group has zero positive observations and standard logistic regression separates.
- H1 and H2 were checked using city-clustered standard errors, models of 20 city means and leave-one-model-out analyses.
- A city-level contextual dataset includes income, WHO region and documented crisis-service availability.

**Evidence:** `tables/City_Level_Context.csv`; `tables/Table_CityLevel_Outcomes.csv`; `tables/Table_14_EffectiveN_Robustness.csv`; `tables/Table_15_LeaveOneModelOut.csv`.

### 5. Test whether the apparent income effect is explained by service availability

**Feedback:** Re-run the analysis using city context and test whether income remains important after crisis-service availability is included.

**Response: Completed.**

- The component analysis shows that the H1 gradient is driven mainly by crisis-contact inclusion.
- Removing the crisis-contact component removes the income gradient (Kruskal–Wallis p = 0.25).
- In the joint model, High versus Low income becomes non-significant (+0.233, p = 0.18), while established service availability remains significant (+0.446, p = 0.012).

**Decision:** The defensible interpretation is that crisis-contact inclusion tracks documented service availability in this sample. The study does not claim a direct causal income effect.

**Evidence:** dissertation sensitivity-analysis section; `tables/Table_H1_Actionability_EXPLORATORY.csv`.

### 6. Separate surface localisation from verified localisation

**Feedback:** A response can look localised while containing incorrect information. Analyse apparent and verified localisation separately.

**Response: Completed.**

- Surface localisation credits detected local details or contacts.
- Verified localisation credits a contact only when it matches a documented service for the stated country.
- Of 490 responses containing a detected contact, 68 (13.9%) contained a verified contact.
- The H2 coefficient has the same direction under surface and verified specifications, but both remain exploratory because measurement reliability was inadequate.

**Evidence:** `tables/Table_H2_SurfaceVsVerified_EXPLORATORY.csv`; `tables/Table_H2_VerifiedContactSummary.csv`; dissertation surface-versus-verified figure and table.

### 7. Strengthen the helpline/contact audit

**Feedback:** Verify extracted contacts and keep verified, incorrect, suspicious, undocumented and unknown categories separate. Do not call every unverifiable number fabricated.

**Response: Completed to dissertation scope, with uneven verification coverage disclosed.**

- The audit contains 466 extracted contact records representing 417 unique country–number pairs.
- Categories are kept separate; the dissertation uses “verified”, “visibly suspicious”, “unresolved” or “unverifiable” rather than treating all unresolved contacts as fabricated.
- Five countries were directly re-verified against primary sources during the final dissertation work. The remaining countries retain documented desk-research classifications.
- Suspicious-pattern counts are described as a lower-bound indicator, because plausible-looking incorrect contacts may not be detected.

**Remaining limitation:** The verification depth is not uniform across all 20 countries. Full independent source verification remains future work.

**Evidence:** `tables/Helpline_Verification_Table.xlsx`; `figures/Figure_Helpline_Verification_Status_Counts.png`; dissertation contact-audit section and Appendix C.

### 8. Perform sensitivity and robustness analyses

**Feedback:** Exclude crisis contacts, use verified contacts, analyse actionability components separately, and check whether results depend on one model.

**Response: Completed.**

- H1 was reanalysed without the crisis-contact component.
- Each actionability component was analysed separately.
- H2 was re-fitted using verified-contact localisation.
- H1 and H2 were re-fitted while leaving out each model in turn.
- City-level and city-clustered analyses were reported to avoid treating 1,120 responses as 1,120 independent geographic units.

**Decision:** Stable coefficients do not rescue invalid measures. Robustness and construct validity are treated as separate requirements.

**Evidence:** `tables/Table_14_EffectiveN_Robustness.csv`; `tables/Table_15_LeaveOneModelOut.csv`; exploratory H1/H2 tables.

### 9. Evaluate modern NLP against the rule-based pipeline

**Feedback addressed during measurement development:** Test whether an automated NLP model performs better than the fixed rules.

**Response: Completed as a diagnostic experiment.**

- `Qwen/Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct` generated seven labels using a frozen few-shot JSON prompt.
- Contextual representations were internal to the transformer; no embedding vectors were extracted, no separate classifier was trained and no fine-tuning was performed.
- On the matched 40-response diagnostic, Qwen showed weak agreement and produced 11 dependency contradictions.
- The experiment did not validate the rule-based pipeline and was excluded from RQ1–RQ3.

**Decision:** The rule-based method remained primary because it is deterministic, traceable and reproducible—not because it was proven perfectly valid.

**Response: Completed.**

### 12. Use conservative interpretation

**Feedback:** Do not equate association with bias, religious variation with stereotyping, or unverifiable contacts with hallucination.

**Response: Completed.**

- “Location-conditioned association” replaces causal language.
- “Religious recommendation/framing” is reported without claiming stereotyping.
- Contact classifications distinguish visibly suspicious, unresolved, unverifiable and verified records.
- The effective geographic sample is acknowledged as 20 cities, even though the response-level dataset contains 1,120 observations.

**Religious-framing qualification:** The quantitative result and selected examples support the description “location-conditioned religious framing”, but the project did not conduct a full qualitative user study of whether that framing is helpful, harmful or stereotypical. That interpretation remains future work.

## Feedback items deliberately not added to the final study

| Suggested extension | Decision and reason |
|---|---|
| Add more cities | Not added. Every additional city requires 56 new model responses plus processing, contact checking and validation. Adding cities at the end would expand the study without repairing measurement validity. The 20-city limitation is reported explicitly. |
| Generate multiple responses per cell | Not completed. The study retains one generation per cell. Repeated generations are future work for testing stochastic stability. |
| Add a second coder | Not possible within the project. The 40-response repeat-coding study evaluates intra-rater consistency only; it is not inter-rater validation. Independent coders are required in future work. |
| Conduct a full qualitative study of religious-framing examples | Not completed. The dissertation reports the regional asymmetry and selected examples but does not infer user benefit, harm or stereotyping. Qualitative user research is required. |


## Main takeaway

Within the selected cities, LLM mental-health responses vary by location, especially in crisis-contact inclusion, surface localisation and religious recommendation. However, the strongest methodological result is that plausible automated measures can produce stable-looking coefficients while failing independent validation. Geographic auditing must therefore combine transparent extraction, human validation and factual verification of local resources.

## Final limitations

- Only 20 deliberately selected cities were studied; global generalisation is not justified.
- Income, WHO region, culture and service infrastructure are strongly confounded.
- Prompts were English-only and contained a minimal location cue.
- One response was generated per model–scenario–city cell.
- Human coding was performed by one researcher; repeat coding showed instrument drift.
- Actionability and surface localisation failed the fresh hold-out criteria.
- Contact verification coverage was uneven across countries.
- The study evaluates response content, not clinical effectiveness or patient outcomes.

## Ethical scope

All prompts are synthetic. No patients, real service users or human participants were included. The outputs must not be treated as medical advice or as a verified directory of crisis services.
