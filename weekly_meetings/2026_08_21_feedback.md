## Overall assessment

The project is now more scientifically honest and much stronger than before.

The strongest part of the dissertation is no longer simply that “LLM responses vary by geography”. The stronger contribution is that you show how difficult it is to audit geographic variation properly. Some results looked strong at first, but fresh validation showed that the measures were not reliable enough. This is an important research finding.

A credible and mature story is therefore:

> LLMs appear to adapt mental-health advice to location, but surface measures of localisation and actionability can be misleading. Reliable geographic audits need human validation and factual verification of local resources.

## What is currently strongest

### Contribution 1

Your strongest result is the safety finding around crisis contacts. Models sometimes provide crisis numbers that look specific and helpful, but many are unresolved or visibly suspicious, especially in countries where no documented national service exists.

This is important because it changes the question from:

> “Do models localise advice?”

to:

> “Do models provide safe and verified localisation, or do they create the appearance of local knowledge?”

which is a stronger research question.

### Contribution 2

Your second strong contribution is methodological. You show that reproducible rule-based metrics can still measure the wrong thing. In other words, a pipeline can be perfectly reproducible and still scientifically weak if the measurement is not validated. This is a valuable lesson for LLM audit research.

Your religious-framing result is also useful, but it must remain cautious. It shows location-conditioned cultural framing. It does not yet prove stereotyping, harm or inappropriate advice.

## How to make a stronger dissertation

### Measurement

To make it stronger, you need to make the evidence cleaner.

The biggest issue is measurement. H1 and H2 cannot be presented as confirmed findings because the actionability and localisation measures failed fresh validation. They can still be discussed, but only as exploratory evidence.

If you want H1 and H2 to become stronger, you need to repair the measures, freeze the new definitions, and test them on a new untouched validation sample. Do not tune the metrics and validate them on the same examples.

### Factual verification

The second issue is factual verification. For localisation, it is not enough that a response mentions a city, an institution or a phone number. You need to separate:

- surface localisation: the response looks local;
- verified localisation: the response gives correct and appropriate local information.

This distinction should become central to the dissertation.

### Interpretation

The third issue is interpretation. Avoid strong claims such as “bias”, “stereotyping”, “fabrication rate” or “global inequality” unless the evidence directly supports them. Use more precise language:

- “location-conditioned variation”;
- “surface localisation”;
- “verified localisation”;
- “visibly suspicious contacts”;
- “unresolved contacts”;
- “exploratory association”.

This will make the dissertation more credible.

## What the paper narrative should be

The paper should not be framed as a simple bias paper. A better title would be something like: **When Localisation Fails: Validating Geographic Audits of LLM Mental-Health Guidance**

The main research question could be:

> **Main RQ**. When users disclose their location in mental-health prompts, do LLMs provide genuinely useful and verified local guidance, or do they sometimes create only the appearance of localisation?

This question is stronger than simply asking whether responses differ by city.

The dissertation should tell this story:

1. We vary only the user's location across controlled mental-health prompts.
2. We observe geographic differences in actionability, localisation and religious framing.
3. Human validation shows that some attractive automated metrics fail.
4. The most safety-relevant issue is pseudo-localisation: crisis contacts that look helpful but are unresolved or visibly suspicious.
5. Therefore, future LLM audits must distinguish surface adaptation from verified support.


## What is still missing

- The main missing element is stronger human measurement. All current human labels come from one coder. You need at least a second **independent** coder on a **new sample**.
- You also need a cleaner verification protocol for crisis contacts. Every number should have a clear status: verified real, general emergency, verified incorrect, visibly suspicious, unresolved. Do not treat unresolved as fake.
- You also need to test stability. At the moment, you have one generation per model–prompt–city cell. Because LLM outputs can vary, you should repeat a small subset of generations and check whether the main patterns remain.
- Finally, the dissertation needs a very clear methodology appendix. Another researcher should be able to understand exactly how raw text became each variable, how validation was computed, and how final figures were produced.
