# Weekly Feedback

## Overall assessment

The project has improved substantially. The manual validation, pre-specified F1 threshold, controlled models and city-level robustness checks are all good research practice. The slides are stronger than the meeting discussion suggested.

The main issue is that the project is now carrying two connected but distinct studies:

1. Geographic variation in actionability, localisation and religious framing.
2. Safety failures involving suspicious or unverifiable helpline numbers.

The second finding may be more important, but it is exploratory and not yet fully verified.

## Direction and publication potential

The core study is now a credible geographic audit of LLM-generated mental-health guidance. The current findings suggest:

- a clear income gradient in actionability;
- stronger localisation where real services exist;
- location-conditioned religious recommendations.

This has moderate workshop-paper potential. However, the evidence is limited by the use of only 20 cities and by strong confounding between income, region, culture and service availability.

The helpline finding may have stronger publication potential because it is concrete, safety-critical and directly consequential. However, “suspicious”, “undocumented” and “fabricated” must remain separate categories until every number has been systematically verified.

## Main methodological concern

The composite metrics are defined at a high level:

- **Actionability** $A$: whether responses provide concrete and followable steps.
- **Localisation** $L$: whether guidance is grounded in the stated location.
- **Religious framing**: whether models recommend spiritual or religious support without the user expressing such a preference.

The current results indicate:

- a monotonic income gradient in actionability,
- greater localisation where established crisis services exist,
- substantially more religious framing in some African and Eastern Mediterranean cities than in European or American ones.

However, the lower-level extraction rules are not presented clearly enough.

For each component of actionability, localisation and religious framing, the methodology must specify:

- the exact definition;
- the regex or vocabulary used;
- how recommendation context and negation are handled;
- positive and negative examples;
- known failure cases;
- validation results.

A further conceptual problem is that an invented telephone number currently increases the localisation score. The analysis should therefore distinguish between:

- **surface localisation**: the response appears locally specific;
- **verified localisation**: the named institution or contact is factually correct.

## Communication during meetings

Your technical work is stronger than the way it is currently presented.

Avoid statements such as “the validation is done” or “the model is complete”. For every task, explain:

1. What question you addressed.
2. What data you used.
3. What method you applied.
4. What result you obtained.
5. What the result means.
6. What limitation remains.
7. What decision follows.

Each slide should answer one research question and include the method, result, interpretation and limitation. Do not present isolated implementation details without first explaining their scientific context.
