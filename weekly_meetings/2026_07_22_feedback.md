### Direction of the research

Publication potential is currently low. At this rate, the dissertation shapes up to be a good degree dissertation, however it falls short of the standards required for scientific research. The current analytical direction is still too diffuse. It mixes city, income and regional comparisons without a clear hierarchy of inference.

### Biggest risks

- The main risk is that binary keyword indicators are being treated as valid measures of complex concepts. For example, detecting the word “therapist” does not necessarily mean that the response contains useful professional guidance. Similarly, detecting “family” does not show whether family support is recommended, discouraged or merely mentioned.
- The manual coding has not been completed, so there is no evidence that the automated extraction is accurate.
- Combining geography and income prematurely can erase city-level heterogeneity and create misleading group effects.
- The project risks accumulating many weak indicators instead of testing a small number of meaningful hypotheses.

### Possible resolution

I would reduce the project to three outcome families:

- Actionability. Whether the response provides concrete steps that a user can take. Possible components: immediate action; professional referral; emergency escalation; crisis contact; named coping step.
- Localisation. Whether the advice is appropriately grounded in the stated location. Possible components: correct local number; named local institution; country-specific service; explicit location reference.
- Support orientation. Which type of support is prioritised: professional; family or friends; community; religious or spiritual; self-management.
